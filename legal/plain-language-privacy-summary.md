# TrueVault privacy at a glance

A short summary. It is not a substitute for the full
[Privacy Policy](privacy-policy.md) — it leaves out detail, and detail is where privacy questions
are actually answered.

---

### Files you select

Used to encrypt and manage your private vault. TrueVault reads only the files you pick — it has no
permission to browse your storage.

### Local storage

Vault files are stored in encrypted form on your device, in the app's private storage. Nothing is
uploaded. The app has no internet permission and no server to upload to.

### Secure Copy

Creates an encrypted copy. **Your original stays where it is**, and the app says so rather than
calling the item fully secured.

### Secure Move

The original is removed only after the encrypted copy has been verified and Android has approved the
deletion. If you decline, or the deletion fails, TrueVault tells you the original is still there.

### Duplicate copies

The scanner can find accessible copies of your files, but it cannot guarantee it found every one. It
cannot see other apps' storage, cloud accounts, messages, or anything you already sent to someone.

### Biometrics

Android verifies your fingerprint or face. **TrueVault never receives your fingerprint or face
data** — only a yes or no. Your password still works, and still decides.

### If you forget your password

Your recovery key is the only way back in. **If you lose both, nobody can recover your vault** —
not you, not the developer. There is no account and no reset email.

### Optional diagnostics

There are none. This version of the app contains no analytics and no crash reporting, so there is
nothing to switch off and no consent screen to show you.

### Backups

Backups are local and encrypted. **Uninstalling the app removes your vault** — only a backup you
exported yourself survives.

### Your control

From Settings you can export an encrypted backup, delete individual items, delete all local vault
data, or reset the app entirely.

### What TrueVault does not claim

It is not unbreakable. It cannot protect a compromised device, it cannot reach copies of your files
it has no access to, and it has not been independently audited. The full policy says exactly where
the limits are.
